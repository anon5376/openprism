# Scientific figure design

Write down what a mark represents before choosing the chart. When the manuscript gives two group medians and a paired estimate, keep the medians in a table or on a shared level scale. Plot the reported within-pair estimate on its own contrast scale. Subtracting the group medians can give a different value. Summary statistics alone do not support reconstructing individual points, trajectories, or distributions.

On a difference scale, mark zero and name the subtraction order. On a ratio scale, mark one and choose a scale appropriate to the ratio. Place the estimate and both interval endpoints using the same coordinate transformation. Specify the interval method and level as reported. Extend the axis far enough to show both ends. If an interval crosses the reference, preserve that fact in the figure and the interpretation.

Use position and shape to convey meaning in greyscale. Directly label a single estimate. For several estimates, give them a shared scale when a comparison is intended. Keep units on the axis or in the column heading and print meaningful changes in precision only when supported by the source.

Prefer vector geometry for intervals, lines, and labels in PDFs. At final size, trace the estimate to its tick marks, verify each endpoint against the source, and check that labels clear the marks and page edges. Keep the editable figure source with the manuscript.
